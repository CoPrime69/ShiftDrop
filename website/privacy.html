Policy

Privacy

ShiftDrop works on your files on your PC. It has no accounts, no telemetry and no ads, and it sends nothing about you anywhere. This page lists every way the app, and this website, touch the network.

The app

  • Offline by design. ShiftDrop.exe makes no network connections. The build fails if it, or the archive worker, links a networking library.
  • Your files stay where they are. Every conversion and edit runs on your PC, and each result is saved next to the original. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Video and audio go through a bundled build of FFmpeg that has no network support, and ShiftDrop only lets it open local files.
  • Archives are read by a separate worker in a Windows AppContainer, which can't reach the network or your other files.
  • Copy text (OCR) and face detection use what Windows already has. Nothing is downloaded.
  • What it keeps. Settings, history, recipes and logs stay in %LOCALAPPDATA%\ShiftDrop on your PC. They are never sent anywhere, and you can delete them at any time.

Update checks

Update checks are the one exception. A separate program, ShiftDrop-Updater.exe, makes them; ShiftDrop itself never does.

  • Once a day, and when you press Check now, it sends one request to GitHub, api.github.com/repos/CoPrime69/ShiftDrop/releases/latest, with the user agent ShiftDrop-Updater/<version>. There are no cookies, no sign-in and no identifiers.
  • When you choose to update, it downloads that release's installer and SHA256SUMS.txt from GitHub, and refuses the installer unless its SHA-256 matches.
  • Like any website, GitHub sees your IP address when the request arrives. The GitHub Privacy Statement covers how GitHub handles it.
  • To turn it off, open Settings › Updates and set When an update is available to Off, or put Mode=off under [Updates] in settings.ini. Then nothing goes online unless you press Check now.

This website

  • No cookies, no analytics and no trackers. This site serves its own fonts, pictures and videos; none come from third parties.
  • The live demo runs in your browser. Nothing you do in it is uploaded.
  • To show the latest version and the star count, your browser asks GitHub's public API for the latest release and the repository's details. The download buttons take you to GitHub.
  • Vercel hosts the site. It handles each request and may keep short-lived server logs, as Vercel's privacy policy describes.
  • Your browser's local storage keeps your light or dark theme choice. It never leaves your device.

Changes and contact

If any of this changes, the changelog and this page will say so first. Ask questions on the issue tracker, and report security problems privately as described in SECURITY.md.
